The short version

The information was all there. Finding it was the problem.

Playpen’s existing website is not missing content — it holds almost fifty pages covering history, curriculum, campus, policies and achievements. The difficulty is that a parent has to already know the school in order to navigate it. The redesign reorganises what exists rather than replacing it.

48→14

Fewer pages, more findable

Two pages were a single sentence long. Related information now sits together instead of being scattered.

48→3

Far lighter to load

The current homepage pulls in 48 separate design and script files before a parent sees anything. The new one uses three.

Pages Google can describe

Not one page currently tells search engines what it is about. Every new page does.

What we found

Six things worth explaining.

Finding 01

Too many pages, each holding too little

The site has 48 pages. Some carry a great deal — the Senior School page holds the entire O Level and A Level curriculum. Others carry a single sentence: “Multimedia Projector” and “The Identity Card” are each one short paragraph, yet each occupies a menu slot of its own. A parent hunting for the subject list has no way to guess where it lives.

Before
48 pages. The four school stages sat on four separate pages with no overview tying them together.
After
14 pages. Nothing was deleted — every fact was moved into the section it belongs to, and each is still individually linkable.
Finding 02

The site loads 48 files before a parent sees anything

The existing site is built on WordPress with a page-builder and six plugins. Each adds its own design and script files. We counted 24 stylesheets and 24 scripts on the homepage alone — 48 separate downloads before the page can appear. On a good office connection this is invisible. On a phone on mobile data in Dhaka, it is the difference between a parent waiting and a parent leaving.

Finding 03

Google cannot describe the school

When a page appears in a Google result, the grey line underneath it comes from a short summary written into the page. The Playpen homepage has none, so Google invents one from whatever text it finds first. There is also no structured information — the standard, invisible block that tells search engines “this is a school, here is its address, phone number and founding year.” Without it, Playpen cannot appear properly in map results or knowledge panels.

Before
  • No page summary on the homepage
  • No school information for search engines
  • Page title reads “Playpen School – Playpen Center” — no city, no curriculum
  • Five competing main headings on one page
After
  • A written summary on each of the 14 pages
  • Address, phone, founding year and curriculum supplied to search engines
  • Titles carry the school, the curriculum and Bashundhara
  • One main heading per page
Finding 04

Small errors that quietly cost trust

Two are live on the site today. The homepage banner reads “Inspring young minds”. The photo gallery returns “gallery was not found” instead of photographs. Neither is serious on its own. Both are the kind of thing a parent comparing three schools notices, and quietly files away.

The writing across the site also switches between “students”, “pupils” and “children”, and between “CIE” and “CAIE” for the same examination board. All copy has been rewritten in one consistent voice, and a house style is now documented so it stays that way.

Finding 05

No way to make contact without picking up the phone

The existing contact page lists numbers and email addresses, which is useful — but there is no form. A parent browsing at nine in the evening, which is when parents browse, has nothing to do but write the number down and hope they remember. Every enquiry depends on someone being at a desk between 9:00 AM and 1:00 PM.

Before

Phone numbers and email addresses only. No enquiry form anywhere on the site.

After

Three forms — admissions enquiry, general contact, and alumni registration. They are built and validated but not yet connected; they need pointing at the school’s email or admissions system.

Finding 06

The school’s own history contradicts itself

This one we cannot fix — only Playpen can. The About page says the school was founded in 1977. The same page refers to a kindergarten beginning in 1997. The homepage banner says 48 years; a recruitment page says 49. These cannot all be right.

The redesign uses 1977 throughout, because that is what appears most often and most prominently — but it is flagged as the first item the school must confirm before launch. A school selling its heritage should be certain of its own founding year.

Brand audit

There was an identity. There were no rules.

Playpen has a genuinely good crest — a crimson shield, gold border, five stars, “Established in 1977” on a ribbon. It carries real weight. What did not exist was any written guidance on how to use it, which colours accompany it, or what to do when it is too small to read.

What was missing
  • No brand guidelines document of any kind
  • No defined colour values — colours varied across the site
  • No version of the logo that works as an app icon or profile picture
  • No typefaces specified
  • No rules on clear space, minimum size or backgrounds
  • No photography direction
What now exists
  • Full guidelines covering 11 areas, plus a one-page board
  • Seven colours, each with a defined job and a measured contrast level
  • Seven logo files including a simplified mark readable at icon size
  • Two typefaces, both free to use commercially
  • Clear space, minimum sizes and approved backgrounds documented
  • Photography direction, and a written house style for all copy

The colours came from the crest, not from a designer’s preference

Every value below was sampled directly out of the existing school crest, so the palette carries equity the school already owns rather than introducing something new.

One deliberate addition. The gold from the crest is beautiful but, on a pale background, too faint for many people to read comfortably — it fails the international accessibility standard. Bronze is the same colour family, darkened just enough to pass. Gold is still used everywhere it works: on dark backgrounds, and as fine lines and detail.

Just as important

What we deliberately did not change.

A redesign that throws away a school’s history is not a redesign, it is a replacement. These were kept on purpose.

The crestNot redrawn, not modernised, not simplified. Forty-eight years of recognition stays exactly as it is.
The school’s own words“We assure you of the best possible care and education for your child” still opens the site. So does “Playpen was born with this in mind.”
Every factAll 48 pages were mapped into the new 14. Nothing was dropped — the mapping is documented page by page.
The school’s photographsReal students, real classrooms, real campus. No stock photography was introduced anywhere.
Crimson and goldSampled from the crest rather than replaced. The school looks like itself, only clearer.
Every existing web addressOld links still work. Anyone arriving from an old bookmark or an old Google result lands on the right new page.

Before launch

Five things only the school can answer.

The redesign is complete. It should not go live until these are settled — all five are facts we could find on the old site but could not verify as still true.

Confirm the founding year

1977 or 1997, and whether the site should say a year count at all — it goes out of date every September.

Confirm the subject lists

Twelve O Level and nine A Level subjects are published. A family may choose Playpen because of one of them.

Dial every phone number

Eleven numbers and eight email addresses across five departments are published on the site.

Confirm the admission forms and intake

Which classes are open, and whether the two downloadable forms are the current ones.

Confirm consent for named students

Twelve students are named with their results on the achievements page. The school’s child protection policy requires consent on record.

Two smaller items sit with whoever hosts the site: connecting the three enquiry forms to the school’s email, and testing the site on a real phone rather than a simulated one.

In summary

A modern school that has history — not an old website with a new coat of paint.

Playpen’s problem was never a lack of substance. It was that the substance was hard to reach, slow to load, invisible to search engines, and presented without a single set of rules holding it together. All four are now addressed. What remains is confirmation from the school on a handful of facts, and a decision on where the site will live.
