Student support

The student counsellor, career and university guidance and the health centre, for every class from Playgroup to Class XII, as the school describes them.

01Three servicesCounselling, guidance, health

Who a student can turn to.

  • Student counsellor

    Someone on standby.

    A designated member of the faculty is on standby to answer questions and to give advice and support when a student needs it. The counsellor helps with academic concerns, non-academic issues and behavioural matters that teachers flag.

    How to reach: call the line for your child's class and ask for the counsellor.

  • Career and university counselling

    Guidance for what comes next.

    The Vice Principal for Class VI to XII and the Teacher-in-Charge give guidance on career choices, university applications and the steps for higher study at home and abroad.

    How to reach: call the line for Classes VII to XII and ask for career guidance.

  • The health centre

    Minor health issues, during the day.

    A health care facility on the campus handles minor health issues during the school day. Serious cases are handed over to the parents, so that the family decides on treatment.

    How to reach: call the line for your child's class and ask for the health centre.

From the school's student-support and campus pages.

02At every stagePlaygroup to Class XII

Support looks different at three and at seventeen.

Elementary
Teachers stay with the children through the day. The school says safety and confidence come first in KG I and KG II.
Junior
The school says children in Classes I to III are never put under pressure and learn at their own pace.
Middle and Senior
The counsellor for academic, non-academic and behavioural matters that teachers flag. School policies has the code of conduct.
Senior and A Level
Career counselling, recommendation letters, transcripts and testimonials, and the workshops below.

03For the senior yearsLetters, transcripts, workshops

Papers for applying to college and university.

Senior students receive support materials for their college and university applications: recommendation letters, transcripts and testimonials, with weight on academic results and on activities outside the classroom. The school says it runs workshops and seminars through the year, on the topics below.

Internet safety
Responsible use of the internet and social media.
University applications
Support for applying to international institutions.
Model United Nations
A conference format in which students debate as delegates.
United World College
A group of international schools and colleges that admit students aged 16 to 19, mostly on scholarship; the school runs an information session on them, as listed on its page.
The Duke of Edinburgh's Award
A youth award built on volunteering, learning a skill, physical activity and an expedition; the school lists the programme on its page.

04HealthOn the campus, during the day

Looking after health during the school day.

The school looks after the health of its students with a health care facility on the campus and a sick room. The building has drinking water and fire extinguishers on every floor, and separate toilets for boys and girls on every floor.

When a case is serious, the school hands it over to the parents so that the family can decide on treatment. That is why the school asks families to keep their contact details current.
