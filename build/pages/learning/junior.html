{{no}}What children learn hereEnglish first

A strong start in English, at a child's own pace.

The school says children in Classes I to III are never put under pressure. They learn at their own pace, and build good behaviour, ways of speaking with other people and social manners as they go.

English comes first, and the school treats it as the way into every other subject: Mathematics, Science, Bengali and the rest. What children learn in these three classes prepares them for the next stage.

English
The foundation of the Junior curriculum, taught as the key to learning in every other subject.
Mathematics
Fundamental and logical ideas, beginning with addition and subtraction taught with visual aids.
Bengali
Reading, writing and recitation, which build a child's ability to communicate and an awareness of culture.

Built across these three classes

  • A strong foundation in English
  • Mathematics with visual aids
  • Reading and recitation in Bengali
  • Library lessons
  • Good behaviour
  • Social manners
{{no}}Library lessonsA lesson of its own

Reading has a lesson of its own.

What changes at this stage

Library classes run for every level of the school, and a Librarian looks after them.

The school encourages children to read books, magazines, periodicals and newspapers from the library, to improve both their English and their ideas. The library was modernised for the new campus, and the school bookshop is beside it.

{{no}}ProgressThe first report cards

How progress reaches you.

The school year has two semesters, and a written examination ends each of them. The school uses a range of assessment tools alongside the formal papers and issues Progress Reports. To be considered for promotion, a student must reach the passing marks in the core subjects; the school states this rule for its Junior and Senior sections. Examinations and reporting has the whole year.

{{no}}How to joinThree steps for Classes I to III

Call, sit the test, hear.

  1. 01

    Call or visit.

    Call the line for your child's class, listed above, or come and see the building. The school accepts applications for the current year if there is space, and space changes by class and by year, so call first.

    The Principal and the parents meet for an interview. The result comes by telephone.

{{no}}QuestionsFrom families in Classes I to III

Questions parents ask.

How is progress measured in Classes I to III?

The school year has two semesters, and a written examination ends each of them. The school uses a range of assessment tools alongside the formal papers and issues Progress Reports. To be considered for promotion, students must reach set passing marks in the core subjects. Examinations and reporting has the whole year.

How can I follow my child's progress during the year?

Through the school's parent and student portal, which shows academic performance, class tests, class work markings, homework, report cards, attendance and punctuality. {{ext:portal}}

What happens if my child loses a library book?

The Librarian tells parents through the class teacher when a book has not been returned. If the book still cannot be returned after a set period, the parent replaces it with a similar copy or pays its cost.
