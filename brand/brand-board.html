Design direction

The one we chose: Editorial Heritage

A crest school treated like a good magazine. Warm parchment ground, deep crimson anchors, gold used as a hairline rather than a fill, and an expressive serif doing the talking. Why: Playpen has 48 years of genuine history and a crest to prove it — the design should bank that rather than hide it, while the layout and pacing stay unmistakably current.

Considered: Bright & Playful

Primary colours, rounded sans, illustration. Rejected: it reads as a nursery, and Playpen teaches to A Level. It would undersell the senior school to exactly the parents the site needs to convert.

Considered: Institutional Navy

Navy, grey, stock photography, dense grid. Rejected: it is what every competitor already looks like, and it throws away the crimson-and-gold equity the crest has held since 1977.

Logo system

The crest stays. Everything else is built around it.

The existing shield is the school’s strongest asset and is not being replaced. What it lacked was a system: a mark that survives at 24px, a wordmark that works without the crest, and rules for both. That is what follows.

01 · Heritage crest — primary

Ceremonial use, certificates, uniforms, signage, print. Never below 32mm.

02 · Shield mark — digital

Derived from the crest: same shield, same five stars, same colours. Legible at 24px. Favicons, app icons, avatars.

03 · Wordmark

Fraunces 700 with a gold rule. For contexts where the crest would compete — document headers, email signatures.

04 · Single colour

One ink. Embossing, engraving, stamps, garment transfer, fax.

05 · Horizontal lockup — site header, letterhead, signage

06 · Inverted — dark surfaces and photography

07 · Stacked lockup — square formats, stationery, merchandise

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space on all four sides equal to the height of the shield mark’s crown — the distance from the top edge of the shield to the centre star. Nothing enters that zone: no text, no rules, no photographic detail.

Minimum sizes. Horizontal lockup 180px / 40mm wide. Stacked lockup 120px / 28mm. Shield mark 24px / 8mm. Heritage crest 100px / 32mm — below that, use the shield mark instead.

Do

  • Place the crest or lockup on Parchment, White, Sand, Crimson or Deep Maroon.
  • Use the inverted lockup over photography, and only over the darker part of the image.
  • Swap to the shield mark the moment the crest drops below 100px.
  • Keep the gold gradient intact — it is what makes the shield read as a crest and not a badge.

Don’t

  • Never stretch, rotate, outline, or add a drop shadow to any mark.
  • Never recolour the shield field to anything other than Crimson, Deep Maroon or a single ink.
  • Never set the crest on a busy photograph without a solid colour block behind it.

Colour rules

  • Gold is a hairline, not a fill. Rules, borders, small figures and icons on dark. Large gold fields cheapen the crest.
  • On light backgrounds, gold text becomes Bronze. Playpen Gold only reaches 2.4:1 on Parchment and fails AA.
  • One accent per surface. Crimson carries the action; gold or bronze carries the label. Not both on the same element.
  • Sections alternate Parchment → Sand → Deep Maroon. Never two dark sections in a row.

Never

  • Crimson text on Deep Maroon, or gold text on Parchment — both fail contrast.
  • A gold background carrying white text. Use Deep Maroon text on gold, or invert it.
  • Introducing a colour outside this list — including the brighter reds that crept into the old site.
  • Gradients anywhere except inside the shield mark’s own gold border.

Typography rules

  • Fraunces sets headings only. Never body copy — it tires the eye past two lines.
  • Body copy never smaller than 16px on screen, 10pt in print.
  • Headings get negative tracking; body copy gets none.
  • Use italic Fraunces for the single emphasised phrase in a hero — once per page, no more.

Never

  • A third typeface. Two families is the limit, including in slide decks and PDFs.
  • All-caps Fraunces. It loses everything that makes it worth using.
  • Body text over 68 characters per line.
  • Fraunces below 16px — substitute Plus Jakarta Sans.

Voice

Confident, not boastful.

Playpen has enough real evidence — 48 years, a Cambridge pathway, a purpose-built campus, competition results — that it never needs a superlative. Say the specific thing; let the reader draw the conclusion.

Warm Writes to a parent, not a market. “We assure you of the best possible care and education for your child.” Not sentimental, and never cute about children.
Specific “Junior classes have both a Class Teacher and an Assistant Teacher.” Not “world-class holistic educational ecosystem”.
Plain Short sentences, active voice, one idea per paragraph. Not bureaucratic, and never a notice written as a decree.
Settled Age is stated once and then demonstrated. The school has nothing to prove. Not nostalgic, and not defensive about competitors.

Tone by context

Homepage & admissionsConfident and welcoming. Lead with the child, close with a next step.
Academic pagesPrecise. Subject lists, minimum requirements, dates. No adjectives.
NoticesCalm and operational. What, when, who it affects, what to do.
Policies & conductNeutral and exact. Preserve the official meaning; simplify only the grammar.
AchievementsFactual. Name, class, competition, placing, date. The facts are the boast.
Social mediaLighter, still specific. A real photograph beats a graphic every time.

Words

Use: students · parents · teachers · Cambridge curriculum · O Level · A Level · wellbeing · student support

Avoid: pupils / children / learners used interchangeably · best · No. 1 · world-class · unparalleled · CIE (use CAIE) · guaranteed admission

Signage & environment

Deep Maroon ground, gold hairline, crest at scale.

For gate signage, hall boards and event backdrops: the heritage crest large, a 1pt gold rule, and the descriptor tracked to 0.2em. Nothing else on the surface.

Photography

Real children, real classrooms, real light.

Playpen’s existing photo library is its biggest untapped asset — and the single biggest area needing investment. The images that work are the unposed ones.

Do

  • Children mid-activity — writing, climbing, performing, experimenting — not lined up for the camera.
  • Teachers in frame with students. It is the proof behind “individual attention”.
  • Natural daylight. The campus has good light; use it rather than flash.
  • Wide campus shots that show scale, and close shots that show a face.
  • Caption every photograph, and write alt text that describes what is happening.

Don’t

  • Stock photography of any kind. Parents in Dhaka recognise it instantly.
  • Event banners photographed as the subject — shoot the people in front of them.
  • Heavy filters, HDR, or coloured overlays that fight the palette.
  • Publishing an identifiable student without the consent the school holds on record.

Treatment. Images on dark sections carry a Deep Maroon gradient at 60–95% opacity so type stays legible. Images on light sections are untinted. Corner radius 4px throughout. Aspect ratios: 4 : 3 for cards, 3 : 4 for portraits, 16 : 10 for full-width.
